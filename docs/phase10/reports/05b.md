# Phase 10 · Goal 05b — Luftwaffe models: accurate shapes first

Branch `phase10/05-luftwaffe-models`. The from-scratch shapes of goal 05 are gone.

- **Shapes:** every type is now built on the FlightGear model already in the repository (`tools/convert_models/output/<kind>.glb`, GPL).
- **Kept from goal 05:** parts, controls, UV atlas, bakes, camouflage, markings, weathering, ace liveries and LODs. They are now applied to the FlightGear shape.
- **Shape gate:** an automated silhouette check against public-domain US recognition three-views. All five pass, and the scores are recorded.
- **New pilot figure:** proportioned head and shoulders, leather flying helmet, goggles.
- **KTX2:** GPU-compressed textures with WebP fallback.
- **Checks:** `npm run check` passes: 67 files, 718 tests.

**Pillar 1 (enemy aircraft): 6/10.** Goal 05 claimed 5 with wrong shapes; I would now put that model at 3–4.

## Silhouette gate (the review's main point)

**References.** Public-domain US government three-views (federal works). Page scans come from the Internet Archive and are cached locally, not committed. `tools/hero/luftwaffe/silhouette/refs.json` holds the URLs:
- **Fw 190:** War Department FM 30-30 *Aircraft Recognition Pictorial Manual*, April 1943, p. 112 (A-3 drawing).
- **Bf 110:** FM 30-30, p. 114 (Me 110 C/D).
- **Ju 88:** FM 30-30, p. 118 (Ju 88 A-6, the A-4 airframe).
- **Bf 109 G-6 and Me 262 (revised):** U.S. Army-Navy *Journal of Recognition*, January 1945, p. 15.

**How the check works** (`silhouette/extract.mjs`, `check.mjs`):
- **Reference masks:** ink threshold, then the three largest blobs (front, top, side), then closing and hole fill. Only these binary outline masks are committed.
- **Model side:** the GLB's LOD0 is projected orthographically. Gear and propeller blades are excluded, because the drawings show spinners and retracted gear.
- **Scale:** one uniform scale per sheet, fixed by the **captioned span**. The model is only translated, never scaled per view.
- **Thin details:** wires, masts and barrels are removed from both masks by a 4 cm opening.
- **Scores:** raw IoU, plus a boundary-tolerant IoU that ignores mismatch within 6 cm (print line weight and drawing error).
- **Gate:** tolerant IoU ≥ 0.88 top, 0.85 side, 0.75 front. It runs in `tests/heroModels.test.ts` on the shipped files.

**The gate confirms the review.** The 05 shapes were clearly off: Fw 190 top .926, Ju 88 top .713. The new models track the FlightGear geometry within ±.01 everywhere except the Me 262.

**Me 262.** The FlightGear Me 262 is itself out of proportion: span 10.55 m against the real 12.56 m, length 12.48 m against 10.60 m. It is the one source I corrected.
- **Correction:** a uniform scale to the **published** span and length (x ×1.19, z ×0.849), so the drawing stays an independent check.
- **Not fitted to the drawing:** a z factor tuned to the drawing would score higher (≈ .986 top). The 1945 drawing is itself about 5 % long against its own caption, so I did not use it.
- **Moved with the scale:** every anchor (fans, muzzles, eye, exhausts), plus the Me 262 hit zones (`EnemyDress.ts`) and placeholder size (`Models.ts`).

**Variant notes:**
- The FlightGear Bf 109 is a late G with the Erla hood (G-6 drawing used).
- The Bf 110 drawing is a C/D; the G-2 has larger fins.
- The front views are the weakest evidence: thin projected surfaces and print error.

## What changed

**Shape base: `tools/hero/luftwaffe/fg.py`.** It imports the converted FlightGear glb and welds its loose panels, then runs three steps.
- **Visibility pass:** BVH rays from both sides of every face, from 5 sample points. Faces no ray can reach are deleted (spars, root caps, doubled panels inside the closed skin): 600–3,300 per type. Faces seen only from behind are flipped. Open sheets get a reversed copy 2 mm off.
  - The FlightGear panels have arbitrary winding, and the game's skin is single-sided. Without this pass, wing undersides and tailplanes baked as the wrong surface.
- **Curved refinement:** edges longer than 9–20 cm are split on the PN-triangle curve through their end normals, with the bulge capped at 12 mm.
  - Source vertices never move, so the silhouette cannot shrink. The low-poly cowls and fuselages come out round.
- **Me 262 only:** the scaling described above.

**Parts: `airframe.py` plus the five rewritten `types/<kind>.py`.**
- **Cuts:** plane cuts at the part joints and along every hinge line. `fg.Hinge` defines each control surface by its hinge line, square end planes and depth.
- **Face assignment:** each face goes to a node of the `hero-parts.md` contract: `part_*`, `ctl_*` (ailerons, flaps, outer flaps on twins, elevators, rudder or twin rudders), nacelles, interior. It also gets a paint class, from the source material or region.
- **Panel lines:** their coordinates are measured on the FlightGear surface. `BodyMap` gives station plus angle round the sliced section centre; `LiftMap` gives span plus chord fraction between the sliced leading and trailing edges.
  - The painter recomputes them **per texel**, so panel lines stay straight on long source triangles.
- **Unchanged:** every node name, the anchors and the LOD structure, so `HeroLook`, `Wrecks` and the tests still apply.

**Build fixes found on the way** (they also affected goal 05):
- **AO and position bakes** now run on one joined proxy. In a multi-object bake, Blender spread each object's margin over the other objects' islands, which left speckles of foreign paint. The bakes are also 25× faster.
- **Per-face flags** (paint class, winding) are now stored as mesh attributes, so `mesh.validate()` can no longer shift them.

**Pilot / crew (`parts.pilot`).**
- **Body:** a real-proportion head (16 × 22 × 20 cm) with jaw and nose; neck; a lofted torso with sloping 46 cm shoulders; jacket collar; parachute harness.
- **Helmet:** leather, down to the nape, with ear cups and a chin strap.
- **Goggles:** pushed up on the brow, with frames, lenses and a strap.
- **Other details:** gauntlets on stick and throttle.
- **Textures:** the crew now gets full atlas density (they used to be at 45 %).
- See [pilot shots](../shots/05b/after-turntable-fw190-pilot.jpg) and the [Bf 109 pilot](../shots/05b/after-turntable-bf109-pilot.jpg). It reads as a WWII airman through the canopy. It is still a stylised, low-detail figure with faceted shading up close.

**Paint.** Unchanged except for one adjustment: isolated chipping and the walkway wear were toned down, because at gun range they read as snow.

**KTX2.**
- **Encoder:** Basis Universal 2.50.0 from goal 02b (`/private/tmp/b17-encoder/.../basisu`, passed as `BASISU=`). Nothing was installed.
- **Files:** `finish.mjs` writes `public/models/<kind>_{base,normal,orm}.ktx2`: UASTC 4×4 with RDO, Zstandard, full mip chain, colour in sRGB, normal and ORM linear.
- **Fallback:** the GLB keeps its embedded WebP maps.
- **Runtime:** `HeroLook.enableHeroKtx2(renderer)` is called once the Engine exists (hook in `main.ts`). It swaps the shared skins' maps per type at background download priority. On Low it swaps the base only; normal and ORM stay off, as before.
  - If a download or transcode fails, the WebP stays, with one console warning.
- **Transcoder:** three's Basis transcoder ships in `public/models/basis/` (Apache 2.0, licence included).
- **Browser results** (headless shell):
  - All five types end up on compressed maps (ASTC 4×4, 12 mips) on High and Low, a moment after the menu.
  - With every `.ktx2` returning 404, all five keep WebP and the page shows no errors.
- **GPU memory:** about 34 MB of RGBA per type before, about 8.4 MB as ASTC/BC7 (estimate: 1 byte per texel plus mips).

**Other edits:**
- **Credits** (`public/models/CREDITS.md`): the five files are now credited as GPL FlightGear derivatives (the shapes) with our own textures and parts. They are no longer "own work".
- **Docs:** `docs/phase10/hero-parts.md` and the pipeline `README.md` are updated.
- **Hit zones:** the Bf 110 and Ju 88 wing zones moved onto the real chord (they sat up to 1.5 m forward).

## Pillar 1 self-score: 6/10

What is now right:
- **Proportions:** the five types have their real proportions. They match period three-views as closely as the FlightGear sources do, and the Me 262 is better than its source.
- **Recognisable features:** cowls, canopies, tails and planforms read correctly up close and at 150 m.
- **Retained from goal 05:**
  - Readable camouflage, markings and weathering
  - Moving controls on the true hinge lines
  - Named breakable parts
  - Proper crew figures
  - GPU-compressed textures

Still short of 10:
- **Source detail is capped by FlightGear.** Refinement rounds the curves, but intrinsic detail (canopy frames, intakes, fillets) is the FlightGear modellers'. Some joints are crude up close, and the Fw 190's cut-down canopy frames are blocky.
- **Variant mismatches:** the Bf 109 is a late G with the Erla hood rather than the early G-6 canopy, and the Bf 110 fins are the FlightGear version's.
- **Normal maps** are still procedural, not baked from a sculpted high-poly mesh.
- **Crew figures** are stylised.
- **Cut parts have no caps:** a detached wing is open at the cut. A deflected aileron shows a slit, which is barely visible at the ±20° in-game throws.
- **Ju 88 front and side** score lowest (.857 / .886 tolerant). Partly this is the source (the gondola and nose glazing differ from the A-6 drawing), partly the drawing.
