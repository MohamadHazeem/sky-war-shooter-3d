## What changed

**Blender asset pipeline (own-made, `tools/interior/`, one command: `sh tools/interior/build.sh`)**
- `build_crew.py` → `public/models/crew.glb` (171 KB). A skinned B-17 crewman: B-3 sheepskin jacket
  with fleece collar, cuffs and hem, A-3 trousers, A-6 boots, M4 flak helmet over a leather helmet,
  B-8 goggles, A-14 oxygen mask with a corrugated hose to the chest clip, B-4 Mae West, parachute
  harness with brass fittings, and a fitted M1 flak vest (its own mesh, so a man can go without it).
  Body is a skin-modifier hull (subdivided twice) weighted by distance to the bones. Simple 18-bone
  rig. Clips: `idle`, `fire` (braced, 12 Hz shudder), `hit` (jerked back, knees go, collapses),
  `down` (lying wounded, breathing), `help` (kneeling, pressing a dressing), `sit` (radio
  operator writing and looking at his sets), `kneel` (tail gunner). Plus a 12 % LOD body and
  `fpArm` (the player's own forearm and gloved fist). The game recolours the jacket, fleece,
  trousers, helmet and skin per crewman.
- `build_interior.py` → `public/models/b17_interior.glb` (393 KB). It's modelled in the bomber
  frame around the procedural shell:
  - `kitWaist`: zinc-chromate Z-section formers every 0.48 m and hat stringers, broken at the
    windows; window doublers; a plywood walkway with treads; G-1 oxygen bottles in cradles; A-12
    regulators with blinker and gauge, hoses and walk-around bottles; ammunition boxes with feed
    chutes and spare cans; interphone boxes with cords; quilted flak curtains; a hanging flak suit
    and helmet; chest-pack parachutes; a CO2 extinguisher; a first-aid kit; control cables with
    pulleys; hand rails; dome lamps; conduit.
  - `kitRadio`: the table, BC-348 receiver with lit dials, command sets, the BC-375 transmitter
    and tuning units, log book, swivel seat, lamp, roof hatch with daylight, a side window, and the
    forward bulkhead with its door open.
  - `kitBay`: bomb-bay shell, catwalk on its keel truss, racks, and doors with daylight leaking at
    the seams.
  - `kitBombs`: twelve 500 lb bombs, hidden after bombs away.
  - Ambient occlusion is baked into the vertex colours with Cycles, against a stand-in of the shell.
- `compress.mjs` handles colour layer fixing, mesh names, prune and meshopt.
  `preview.py` and `inspect_colors.mjs` are checking aids. See the README there. Both files are
  logged in `public/models/CREDITS.md`.

**Game side**
- `src/game/InteriorAssets.ts`: lazy load (core/Preload, background priority, not waited for),
  material tuning, and the interior bounce light. The bounce is an emissive term multiplied by
  each surface's own albedo and baked AO, set each frame from sky and sun (more when the sun pours
  through a window). It also warms the programs (`World.prewarm`). If the assets arrive after the
  prewarm, `GunnerController.attach` compiles them behind the mission fade.
- `src/game/InteriorKit.ts`: dresses the waist group. It adds the kit, hides the procedural
  details it replaces (`waistLegacyDetail`, `radioLegacy`), and gives the waist shell,
  bulkheads, radio-room shell and tail tunnel the new skin, with UVs that land rivet lines on the
  kit's members. `undo()` restores everything.
- `src/render/InteriorSkin.ts`: a procedural bare-aluminium skin (1024² colour, normal and
  roughness). Each panel has its own tint and finish, plus mill streaks, zinc-chromate
  overspray, lap joints, double and single rivet rows (domed in the normal map), oil-canning,
  scuffs, grime and stencils. Vertex grime is added toward the floor and crown.
- `src/game/CrewActor.ts`: the skinned crewman. It runs an AnimationMixer, cross-fades clips,
  switches LOD, and recolours per look with a shared cache. On the gun there is procedural rig
  work over the clip:
  - two-bone IK puts the hands on the spade grips as the gun tracks fighters
  - he crouches and leans under high targets, with leg IK keeping his feet planted
  - his head pitches along the gun

  No per-frame allocation.
- `src/game/LivingInterior.ts`, interior 2.0 path:
  - The other waist gunner is a CrewActor (`idle` or `fire` with IK).
  - When he is wounded he plays `hit` and slides to lie along the walkway (`down`).
  - After 3.5 s a crewman kneels by him playing `help`. He only appears where the player isn't
    looking. He gives way to the rookie at the gun after 14 s.
  - The radio operator `sit`s at his sets and the tail gunner `kneel`s at his guns (LOD bodies).
    Both play `hit` → `down` when wounded.
  - The cut-down airframe also clears the bomb bay.
- `src/game/InteriorFx.ts`: the sun shafts are now ray-marched. The prism is closed with caps,
  and each fragment clips the view ray analytically against the six planes. Entry faces
  integrate to the exit; exit faces integrate only with the eye inside the beam. So nothing is
  counted twice and no depth texture is needed. Along the ray: two octaves of drifting dust
  noise, bright motes, soft edges, and fading from the window. This covers the waist windows and
  the ball and tail apertures. The classic shader stays for Low.
- `src/game/PlayerHands.ts`: the player's sleeves and gloved fists sit on both spade grips at the
  waist guns and on the outer grips of the tail guns (they ride the recoil). For the top and ball
  turrets there is a small control yoke, placed below the sight picture. You see them when you
  look down (free look, C).
- `src/dev/interiorShots.ts`: `await sws.interiorShots('after')` gives the fixed station views used
  below.

## Pillar 6 self-score (B-17 interior & crew)
Before: 1/10. After: **5/10**.

What it now has:
- real structure, equipment and lighting depth
- people who move, fire, fall, lie wounded and get help
- dusty volumetric shafts
- the player's own hands

What keeps it from 8–10:
- The crewmen are still low-detail up close. The other gunner stands about 30 cm from the
  player's head, because both waist windows are at the same z. The hull is a skin-modifier
  blob with vertex colour and a fabric normal, with no sculpted folds or textures.
- Shafts are visible as haze, but they are subtle in most sun angles.
- The top turret and ball still have the old shells.
- There's no walking locomotion, so the helper appears out of view rather than walking over.
