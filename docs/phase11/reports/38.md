# Phase 11 · 38 — Mod support (JSON): report

Branch `phase11/38-mods-json`. Players can load community content from JSON files: missions, paint schemes
(colours and optional camouflage images), nose art (images) and crew names. A Mods screen turns each mod on
or off. Mods are checked against a fixed schema and never run code.

## What players get
- **Hangar → Aircraft & Armory → Mods** (new entry). The screen has:
  - **Add mod file** (file picker, several files at once), a drop zone for `.json` files, and *Paste mod text*.
  - A result line for each file: "Added Mighty Eighth Extras. It is switched on." or "Could not add
    broken-mod.json:" followed by the problems, each with its field
    (`missions[0].waves[0].count: must be a whole number from 1 to 12.`).
  - **Installed mods**: a card per mod with name, author, description, what it holds
    ("3 missions · 2 paint schemes · 2 nose art designs"), an **On/Off** switch (`role=switch`), **Fly**
    on each of its missions, and **Remove** (asks once more).
  - **Example mods**: two bundled packs to install with one tap (`public/mods/`).
  - *How to make a mod*: what a mod can hold and **Download template** (a valid starter file).
- **Customize** (hangar): paint schemes and nose art of enabled mods appear after the built-in ones, with a
  `MOD` tag. They are free while the mod is on. If the mod is switched off, the bomber goes back to olive
  drab and the name script.
- **In the air**: the player's B-17 wears the mod's scheme and nose art on every preset. A camouflage
  `pattern` is tiled over the hull, and the national markings, numbers and panel shading stay (shots below).
- **Crew names**: the crew screens show the mod's names and nicknames. Recorded radio calls keep the
  original voices and names. Switching the mod off restores the originals.
- **Mod missions** are flown like Mission Editor missions: briefing → flight → debrief. They use one
  progress id and are never posted to the online leaderboards.
- Full Arabic (RTL) and French/German/Spanish. The screen works at phone size (390×844) with no horizontal
  scroll.

## How it works
- `src/game/Mods.ts` (new, pure): the schema checker, `parseMod` / `validateMod`, the template and the
  installed-mod store (`sws3d.mods.v1`).
  - **Sandbox:**
    - Files are read with `JSON.parse` only.
    - Every field is copied by name into a fresh object. Unknown fields are reported as warnings and
      dropped, and `__proto__` and other inherited keys are never merged.
    - Text is NFC-normalised, with control characters, bidi embeddings/overrides/isolates and `< >` removed,
      and capped in length. Crew names allow letters, marks, spaces and `' . -` only. The screens escape
      all text again.
    - Colours must be `#rrggbb`. Images must be `data:image/(png|jpeg|webp);base64,…` (no SVG and no web
      addresses, so a mod cannot reach the network). Each image is capped at about 220 KB and 1024 px a
      side. Images are decoded as plain bitmaps; any that fail are skipped and the card says so.
    - Missions go through the Mission Editor's encoder and decoder. A mod mission is therefore exactly as
      constrained as a shared code: fixed lists, one `user-mission` id, board `''`.
    - Limits: 1 MB per file, 12 mods, 3 MB in storage. Stored packs are validated again on every load, so
      tampered entries are dropped.
  - Errors are `{ key, vars }` with English templates that `tx()` translates.
- `src/game/ModRuntime.ts` (new): `initMods()` / `setInstalled()` / `applyMods()`. It:
  - renames crewmen on `MEN` (first, last, nick, bio; `call` and `voice` are untouched), restoring the
    originals first;
  - fills the render registry;
  - decodes the images, then refreshes the player's bomber.
- `src/render/ModArt.ts` (new, no imports): the registry of active mod paints and nose art that
  Paint/NoseArt/Customize read, plus `isModKey` and a safe swatch CSS builder.
- **Paint** (`src/render/Paint.ts`): a mod scheme recolours the skin texture on a canvas, as the built-in
  schemes do.
  - Olive-drab pixels take the tiled pattern (or the top colour), grey pixels take the underside colour,
    and everything else (markings, numbers) is kept. Shading is preserved.
  - The hero B-17's streamed maps are GPU-compressed, so it recolours the hero's readable boot atlas
    (512²), kept by `HeroTextures`. The streamed normal/ORM maps still apply.
  - If no readable atlas is available, a colour multiplier is used instead.
  - Materials get no new texture slots, so there are no shader recompiles.
- **Nose art** (`src/render/NoseArt.ts`): a mod design draws its image fitted into the 512×256 decal, with
  the name script under it when `showName` is set. `forgetModNoseArt()` drops cached mod textures and
  thumbnails when mods change or images finish decoding.
- Format documentation: `docs/MODDING.md`. Examples: `public/mods/eighth-extras.json` (3 missions, a
  desert camouflage scheme, Night Black, 2 nose art designs) and `public/mods/hometown-crew.json` (10 crew
  names). Both are generated by `node tools/mods/build_examples.mjs`, which has a tiny rasteriser and PNG
  encoder, so it needs no image tools.

## Hook lines in shared files
- `src/game/Game.ts`: `import { initMods } from './ModRuntime';`, and `initMods();` as the first line of the
  constructor (before any bomber is dressed).
- `src/game/MenuModel.ts`: `['mods', 'Mods', 'Missions, paint and crews made by players']` in the Aircraft &
  Armory tab (4 entries, under the 6-entry cap).
- `src/game/SimpleMenu.ts`: a `case 'mods'` that opens `showModsScreen` (Fly → briefing → back to Mods).
- `src/game/Customize.ts`: `owns()` also returns true for `isModKey(key)`, and nose-art metalness follows a
  metal mod scheme.
- `src/ui/CrateUI.ts` (Customize section): mod nose art and paint tiles after the built-in ones; the preview
  swatch includes mod paints.
- `src/ui/crate.css`: one rule for the `MOD` tag.
- `src/world/HeroTextures.ts`: it records each hero material's boot colour map (`heroBootMap`), and the
  streamed map does not replace a material flagged `userData.modMap`.
- `src/render/Paint.ts`, `src/render/NoseArt.ts`: the mod branches described above.

## Tests
- `tests/mods.test.ts` (44 tests):
  - **Examples, index and template:** every bundled example and the template are valid with no warnings,
    and the index lists exactly the shipped files.
  - **Missions:** a mission given by fields equals the same mission given as an editor code; defaults apply
    to omitted fields; a built mod mission has the user-mission id and no leaderboard.
  - **Rejected input (34 cases):**
    - format, version and id problems; a missing or too-long name; an empty mod;
    - too many entries and duplicate ids;
    - an unknown enemy, target, weather or flak value; out-of-range or fractional counts; no waves or too
      many waves; a bad mission code;
    - CSS injection in a colour; a paint scheme with no colour; out-of-range scale; a non-boolean `metal`;
    - web, SVG and quote-injected image URLs; oversized and missing images;
    - unknown and `__proto__` crewmen; bad crew names.
  - **Bad files:** files that are not JSON, empty or too large.
  - **Cleaning:** HTML, bidi and control characters in text; unknown-field warnings; no prototype pollution.
  - **Fuzz:** 400 mutated example files. Nothing throws, and everything accepted is valid (codes decode,
    colours and images match the whitelist).
  - **Store:** install, replace (keeps on/off), the 12-mod cap and the storage round trip; tampered stored
    entries are dropped.
  - **Runtime:** crew names on/off/restore (radio `call` kept); paint and nose-art registry; `owns()` and the
    active missions; reload from storage.
  - **Menu and languages:** the Mods hangar entry. Every problem text, screen string and example name has
    Arabic, French, German and Spanish, with matching placeholders.
- `npx tsc --noEmit` is clean. `npm run check` passes locally: 122 files, 1401 tests. GitHub CI: see the branch run.
- **Browser check:** `tools/mods_check.mjs` runs Playwright's bundled headless shell (never `channel:
  'chrome'`) against my own Vite on port 5338 and writes `docs/phase11/shots/38/check.json`. It confirms:
  - both examples install from the screen, with 3 Fly buttons;
  - a broken file is rejected and its 5 problems are listed;
  - switching *Hometown Crew* off and on changes Dawson Joe ⇄ Lyle, and the crew screen shows the new names;
  - the Customize mod tiles select the mod nose art and paint;
  - on the High and Low presets the player's B-17 has the `noseArt` decal and mod-painted skin materials;
  - the autoplay bot flew the mod mission "Black Thursday" to *Mission complete*;
  - there is no horizontal scroll (EN/AR desktop, EN/AR phone) and there are no page errors.

## Notes / not done
- **Quality gate:** the live game has no mod support, so a before/after capture-rig comparison doesn't
  apply. For the paint, I compared the stock olive hero B-17 at the same angles (not committed). The mod
  scheme keeps every marking the stock skin shows.
- On High, a mod scheme recolours the hero's 512² boot atlas instead of the streamed 2048² colour map,
  which is GPU-compressed and can't be read back. Panel detail still comes from the full-resolution
  normal/ORM maps, but markings are a little softer than on the stock skin up close.
- The debrief of a mod mission uses the Mission Editor's wording ("The mission you built is flown.") and
  offers the editor's rating strip; the mission is also added to *Your missions* in the editor library.
- Crew bios stay the originals unless the mod sets `bio`. Radio calls keep the recorded names by design.
- Mods are local to the browser (localStorage); there is no online mod sharing. A mission inside a mod can
  still be shared as an editor link.
- Playwright isn't in this repo's `node_modules`. The check ran with `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` (the npx cache) and
  `PLAYWRIGHT_EXECUTABLE` (the bundled chromium headless shell 1243), outside `tools/heavy.sh`: one headless
  page at a time, the dev server stopped afterwards.
