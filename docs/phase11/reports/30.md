# Phase 11 · 30 — Museum and codex: report

Branch `phase11/30-museum-codex`. The Phase 5 aircraft recognition gallery is now a **Museum**
(Hangar → Aircraft & Armory → Museum). It keeps the 3D hangar turntable and the recognition quiz,
and adds history, crew positions and collectable facts for every aircraft, plus a **Codex** with crew
roles, medals and operations. It only uses models already in the game.

## What changed
- **All 11 aircraft from the brief are on the turntable.** I added the P-47D and Spitfire Mk IX to
  the gallery. They use the escort models (`p47d`, `spitfire9`) and the same parameters as in
  `world/EscortTypes.ts`. Their spec cards use real figures. The other types that were already there
  stay (Ju 88 G, He 111, Do 335 and the fictional Luftkreuzer), so the museum has 16 entries.
- **The aircraft card has four sections:** Specs · History · Crew · Facts.
  - Specs is the existing card.
  - History is a short paragraph.
  - Crew lists the positions on board; the B-17 has all ten.
  - Facts holds four facts per aircraft. The first is always open; the others unlock as you play:
    - Enemies: meet the type in a mission, then shoot down 3, then 10.
    - Escorts: fly with them in 1, 3 and 6 missions.
    - B-17: fly 1, 5 and 10 missions.
    - Boss: bring down 1, 2 and 3.

  Locked facts show how to open them ("Shoot down 3"). The section you pick stays selected when you
  switch aircraft.
- **New facts are marked.** An amber dot appears on the aircraft list entry and on its Facts tab
  until you open that aircraft's facts.
- **Encounters are logged during missions.** `World.spawnEnemy` and the allied-fighter factory call
  `encounters.note(type)`, which is a single Set add. The log is armed only in
  `MissionFlow.startMission`, so the menu scene's fighters don't count. At the debrief each type
  counts once per mission, and if the mission opened new facts, the debrief shows a line such as
  "MUSEUM — New facts: Fw 190, Me 262".
- **Codex tab** (DOM panel over the turning aircraft) with three shelves:
  - **Crew roles** (9): pilot, co-pilot, navigator, bombardier, engineer / top turret, radio
    operator, ball, waist, tail. Each has its history and what the role does in this game.
  - **Medals** (all 10 from `Career.MEDALS`): ribbon, history, how to earn it, and how many you hold.
    Real US decorations and the game's own awards are labelled differently.
  - **Operations** (all 12 campaign missions): what really happened on that date. A file opens once
    you have flown the mission (`career.campaignWins` or the mission's medals); until then it shows
    "Classified".
- The Hangar menu entry is renamed **Museum** — "Aircraft, crew, medals and operations".
- **Arabic:** every new string is in the new table `src/i18n/ar/museum.ts`, about 170 phrases. RTL
  works: the codex columns swap sides, as the gallery's do.

## Review round 1: fixes
These are the four required fixes from the quality review.
1. **Air force on the card.** `AircraftSpec` now has a `service` field (`'USAAF' | 'RAF' | 'Luftwaffe'`).
   `serviceLabel()` in `src/game/AircraftData.ts` builds the header line, replacing the hard-coded
   `sideLabel` in `GalleryScreen.ts`. The Spitfire Mk IX now reads **"Allied · RAF"**; in Arabic it is
   "الحلفاء · سلاح الجو الملكي البريطاني". The B-17, P-51 and P-47 stay "Allied · USAAF". This is
   tested in `tests/museum.test.ts`.
2. **The new-fact dot clears when the facts are opened.** `setCurrent` now calls `factsSeen` (which
   runs `markKnown` and refreshes the list) *before* it draws the section tabs. The amber dot is
   therefore gone from both the Facts tab and the list entry while the facts are on screen. Before,
   the tabs were drawn first, so the dot only cleared on the next redraw. `tools/museum_check.mjs` now
   checks this (`factsFresh: false` in every capture).
3. **Phone: the card shows when there is more to read.** When the card is taller than its space,
   it gets a `more` class. A sticky gradient then fades the bottom edge and shows "▾ Scroll for more"
   ("مرّر للمزيد"). The class is updated on render, on scroll and on resize, and is removed at the
   bottom of the card. A section change scrolls the card back to the top. The fade takes no layout
   space, so the scroll height doesn't change when the class toggles.
4. **Facts checked against sources.** I reviewed every date, production figure and first flight in
   the spec cards, the museum facts and the codex (operations, medals) against the standard reference
   figures. The doubtful points I looked up online: the Mk IX first flight and production
   (historyofwar.org, spec sheets), the NA-73X build time (Wikipedia, thisdayinaviation.com) and the
   Werfer-Granate 21 (Wikipedia). Corrections:
   - The spec row "First flight" is now **"Type first flight"** ("أول طيران للطراز"). The dates are
     those of the first prototype of each type: B-17 Model 299, NA-73X, XP-47B, Fw 190 V1 and so on.
   - The Spitfire reads **"5 March 1936 (Mk IX: 1942)"**. 5 March 1936 is the K5054 prototype. The
     first Mk IX conversion flew in early 1942, but sources give either 26 February or April, so only
     the year is shown.
   - P-51: "designed and built in just over a hundred days" is now "rolled out 102 days after the
     contract was signed" (NA-73X, 9 September 1940; first flight 26 October 1940).
   - Bf 110: the 21 cm rockets were "adapted army mortar rounds". They are now described as adapted
     from the army's **Nebelwerfer** barrage rockets (Werfer-Granate 21).
   - These were confirmed unchanged:
     - production: B-17 12,731; P-51 about 15,000 (D model about 8,000); P-47 15,636; Spitfire
       more than 20,000 (Mk IX 5,653–5,665); Fw 190 about 20,000; Bf 109 about 34,000; Ju 88 about
       15,000; Me 262 about 1,430; He 111 more than 6,500; Me 410 about 1,160–1,190;
     - first flights: Bf 109 29 May 1935, Bf 110 12 May 1936, Ju 88 21 December 1936, He 111
       24 February 1935, Me 262 18 April 1941 / 18 July 1942, Do 335 26 October 1943;
     - the twelve operation dates and figures: Emden H2S, 27 September 1943; Bremen, about 30 lost;
       Black Thursday, 60; Kiel, the first P-51 escort; Berlin 6 March 1944, 69 lost;
       Oschersleben, 64; and the rest;
     - the medal years: Air Medal 1942, DFC 1926, Purple Heart revived 1932, Aerial Gunner badge
       1943.
   Arabic was updated for every changed string.
